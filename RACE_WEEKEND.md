# Ultimate F1 Manager: the race weekend (build history and loose ends)

The race weekend was planned and built in steps, W1 to W7. This file keeps what each step built, the principles behind it and what is still to do. How the weekend works today is described in DESIGN.md section 5.10.

## Principles

1. **The car decides most of it; the pit wall decides the margins.** A player who reads the numbers gains a fraction of a place to about a place per car per race over the strategist, depending on its rating; a careless one loses about as much. No single call should be worth more than about three places, except the gambles.
2. **Investment buys information.** A strong Chief Strategist gives tighter weather forecasts, estimates and judgement; strong race engineers, a good simulator and a driver with high Feedback give clearer setup feedback. A weak team plays the same weekend with fuzzier numbers.
3. **Risk flips with position.** A backmarker can gamble on an early stop under a safety car or slicks on a drying track; a leader covers.
4. **Every call is explained.** The debrief says what each call was worth, from the race replayed with the strategist's choice on the same luck.
5. **Skippable, except the race.** Practice and qualifying can each be left to the team; within a live weekend the race is always played, though the strategist can take the calls while it plays on. A whole weekend can still be quick-simmed from Home.
6. **No reload-scumming.** Random rolls are fixed per car, per lap, per weekend from the save's seed, so the same choices give the same race. Different choices change only what they touch: boxing a lap earlier does not change whether a rival crashes.

## Built, in order

- **W1, the simulation** (`src/engine/weekend.js`): the race as a state machine that can be stepped and saved part-way: tyres with wear and a cliff, wet tyres, track conditions that change during the race, real pit stops (cheaper under a safety car or virtual safety car), wing level, standing orders, rival strategies from the tyre model, a race-to-race chaos factor, and random rolls keyed per car. The finished result keeps its old shape, so standings, money, sponsors, driver form and honours work unchanged.
- **W2, the live race** (`src/engine/live.js`, `src/ui/LiveWeekend.jsx`, `src/ui/PitWall.jsx`): the briefing (forecast, wing), the grid (start tyres, lap one), the race with pit-wall calls and estimates, a Box button and standing orders on each car, team radio, hand over at any point, and a debrief valuing each call. Weekend objectives, driver ratings, the rival head-to-head and the gap-to-front history (`src/engine/highlights.js`). The dark pit-wall theme with a light option, team colours, five sections with a bottom bar on a phone, and Home as the pit wall. An involvement setting (on the pit wall or quick sim) with a one-weekend override. Season-1 fixes: the player's starting car 5 points better in Aero and Chassis, and smaller objective rewards. Checks: `tests/w2-check.js`.
  - The live track map (`src/ui/RaceMap.jsx`, outlines in `src/ui/data/trackMaps.js` from the MIT-licensed bacinger/f1-circuits), with your two drivers in their own colours throughout.
  - Moments (`src/ui/Moments.jsx`, `src/ui/raceMoments.js`): banners and full-screen moments on a race weekend (lights out, safety car, red flag, rain, a car out, team-mates colliding, the podium places, the flag, Q2, Q3, the front row and pole), after the race (points, a podium, a win or a one-two, a clean sweep of the objectives), over the season (a title fight, the decider, the championship, last year's rival, the season review) and off the track (into the red, a crisis, the owner's verdict, signings and departures, a new title sponsor).
  - A red flag: about one race in thirteen, likelier after a multi-car crash or in heavy rain, at most once. The field lines up behind the leader and every car may change tyres for free.
- **W3, knockout qualifying**: Q1, Q2 and Q3 with one late run or two, a normal or big-risk lap, a tow between your cars, four new sets of Softs for the whole of qualifying, track evolution, late yellow and red flags (with a restart for some cars left with no time), and qualifying that can be wet, drying or caught by rain. The strategist suggests a plan for each car and says how close it is to the cut, as precisely as its rating allows. Checks: `tests/w3-check.js`.
- **W4, practice** (`src/engine/practice.js`): wing and balance dials for each car from the engineers' setup, three runs a car with the driver's feedback and a lap time, or a long run for a little less tyre wear in the race. Practice and qualifying can each be left to the team in the settings or skipped for one weekend. Checks: `tests/w4-check.js`.
- **W5, folding in the A/B deck** (`INCIDENT_DAY` in `decisions.js`): race-weekend cards the live weekend covers are not drawn; the other 23 come up as incidents on the day they would happen and hold the weekend until answered. The strategist's pick is a judgement call that a weaker strategist sometimes gets wrong, while the estimates come from the team's simulations on shared futures. `tests/w5-strategist.js` measures what the pit wall is worth. Checks: `tests/w5-check.js`.
- **W5.5, holes that broke the spell**: an incident's chance is rolled when it is answered but told only when it happens on track (`timed` in `data/deck.js`): a stalled or strong launch, fuel saving, a failed wing actuator, the stewards' tyre-pressure penalty, an exclusion for plank wear. A car retiring with a known fault retires with that fault about as often as the fault is to blame. A practice crash ends that car's Friday. Friday can be wet. The timing tower shows a lapped car as "+1 lap". Teammates boxing together queue in the box, so strategists split planned stops. Checks: `tests/w55-check.js`.
- **W6, lap by lap**: the race is stepped one lap at a time, with calls on the lap things happen and the Box button and orders acting on the next lap. A safety car stays out three to five laps and a VSC two or three, with every car on the same slow lap. A car that catches the one ahead tries to pass every lap, on their real pace difference, and each fight for position has its own luck, so options compared on the same simulated futures stay a fair comparison. Staying out when a planned stop is due puts it back three laps. Re-tuned: passing (`passTries`), the safety-car lottery and the strategist's judgement (60% right at a rating of 60, 80% at 80, 96% at 95). All six M1 calibration measures are in band; a race takes about 3 ms in the quick sim, so the pre-season projection runs 100 seasons instead of 150. Over 240 races a player who always takes the best estimate gains about 0.65 places per car over a 60-rated strategist (0.75 over 40, 0.4 over 80, almost nothing over 95), and a random one loses about 0.65.
  - **Qualifying as it happens** (`beginQualiSession` and `endQualiSession` in `weekend.js`; `startSession`, `setLateRun` and `finishSession` in `live.js`): each session plays against its clock on the timing tower. The banker laps come in first, then the pit wall decides each car's final run (go again and how hard, or stay in to save a set), then the final laps, any late flag and the knockouts. Splitting a session never changes its result. Checks: `tests/w6-check.js`.
- **W7, a speed profile on the map** (`src/ui/speedProfile.js`): each circuit's outline is scaled to its real length; cornering speeds from each corner's radius are limited by acceleration and braking, tuned so the simulated laps come within about 3% of real lap times. Cars move evenly in time and are drawn where that share of the lap puts them, so they brake into corners and accelerate out while the tower's gaps stay in seconds. About 5 seconds a lap at 1×, up to 16×. The track is tinted by speed, and the Speed button on the map colours it in full.
- **Clean-up after W7**: the race state counts laps (`state.lap`, plans and stops by lap) instead of steps, unused helpers are gone, and a race saved part-way by an earlier build is converted to laps when the save is loaded (`upgradeRaceState`). Then qualifying moved into `src/engine/qualifying.js`, the weekend screens into a file a phase (`BriefingScreen.jsx` to `RaceScreen.jsx`, shared pieces in `weekendParts.jsx`), and small helpers into `src/engine/util.js`.

| Step | Contents | Playable result |
|---|---|---|
| W1 Engine | Tyre model, pit stops, pace modes and wing level in a steppable race; rival strategies; quick sim with calibration unchanged; mid-weekend saves | Same game, new simulation underneath |
| W2 Live race | Race screen, pit-wall calls, hand over to the strategist, debrief with call values | The race becomes interactive |
| W3 Saturday | Knockout qualifying with run plans | Qualifying becomes interactive |
| W4 Friday | Setup in practice, long runs | The full weekend |
| W5 Folding in | A/B weekend cards retired or turned into incidents; objectives; a scripted expert strategist to measure what good calls are worth | Balanced and finished |
| W5.5 Holes | Incident outcomes told when they happen; known faults blamed; practice crashes; wet Fridays; "+1 lap"; double-stacking | The screen and the story agree |
| W6 Lap by lap | The race stepped one lap at a time; qualifying as it happens; calibration re-run | The race as close to live as the game gets |
| W7 Speed profile | Cars brake into corners and accelerate out; slower playback; the track coloured by speed | The map looks like a race |

Each step kept the M1 to M5 checks passing, added its own, and re-ran the calibration and the pit-wall measure where the simulation changed.

## Not built yet

- **Pit lanes by hand.** The map draws every pit lane on the inside of the main straight, which is wrong at some circuits. The outline data has no pit lanes, OpenStreetMap (which has them) could not be reached from the build environment, and which side each one is on needs checking against a real source rather than memory.
- **Pit-wall calls not modelled:** traffic at the pit exit, floor damage and retiring a car to save its parts, a rear gunner holding up a rival, serving a penalty or giving a place back, a late free stop for Softs, and a driver asking to push or to stretch a stint.
- **Resources across races:** power units across the season (rivals take random engine penalties; the player only through a card), tyre sets saved in qualifying carried into the race, and crash damage from pushing coming out of the budget.
- **Weather:** a radar warning before rain arrives, rain coming more than once in a race, and a wet race drying out fully.
- **Practice, kept simple on purpose:** long runs do not sharpen the strategist's tyre estimates (they lower wear instead), and setup cards do not change setup skill when the weekend is run live. Qualifying engine modes are only the Saturday incident card.
- **Screens:** championship progress as a line, the cash forecast as an area chart, tables as lists on a phone, and the season review as a page of its own rather than a full-screen moment.
